# HistoryOut brand

Updated 6 September 2026. Keep the original HistoryOut name, wordmark, favicon and extension icon. Surround that familiar identity with fresh product imagery, a calmer interface and more useful copy. Version 2.0.0 belongs in technical metadata and the changelog.

## The central idea

**Export your browser history. CSV, JSON or HTML.**

HistoryOut turns available browser history into a file people can use: CSV for spreadsheets, JSON for data workflows and HTML for a readable page of links. Users choose their dates, columns and matching pages before exporting.

Export leads acquisition. Customization explains why the file is useful. Daily recaps and saved views are supporting reasons to return between exports. Never claim to measure attention, productivity, billable time or a complete workday.

## Daily usefulness and retention

The recurring loop is: choose Today or Yesterday, click Preview, reopen a useful page, and return later through a saved view. A weekly or project export is an optional outcome of that same loop. There is no need to export every day for the product to be useful.

Use the actual control names in walkthroughs:

- **Today's recap / Yesterday's recap:** visits, pages and sites in the loaded date range.
- **Most visited sites:** the top three sites by visit count, clickable to filter the trail.
- **Your recent trail:** newest-first matching pages, with safe web links that reopen in a tab.
- **Save view / Your saved views:** named local range, filter and export settings. Selecting a saved view restores the settings; Preview or export reads current history.
- **Refresh:** reads the history again to include new visits.

Retention is a hypothesis to measure through voluntary feedback and aggregate store trends. Do not claim the recap has already improved retention. Keep history reads user-initiated and do not add streaks, surprise notifications or background tracking to create artificial engagement.

## Browser-neutral product, intentional search language

Use **browser history** in the brand, hero, interface and general product descriptions. Chrome, Edge and Brave are initial Chromium targets. Public browser support claims must follow the dated compatibility evidence, not just the presence of a browser logo.

It is appropriate to use **export Chrome history**, **Chrome history to CSV**, or **Better History alternative** in a relevant guide, comparison or search-focused tutorial. These phrases answer real queries and describe the current distribution context. Do not repeat them across the general brand or multiply thin pages by substituting a browser name.

The Chrome Web Store name should appear when it identifies the actual download destination. Do not show an Edge Add-ons badge unless there is a real published listing. Do not imply Firefox, Safari, mobile or universal browser support from the Chromium build.

## Copy boundaries

Say “all available history,” “latest matching visit,” “local settings,” “preview matching rows” and “CSV for spreadsheets.” Explain that a saved view is settings, not an immutable archive. URL cleanup removes query strings and fragments; it does not anonymize titles or paths.

Avoid “unlimited history,” “recover deleted history,” “native Excel workbook,” “complete work log,” “time tracker,” “anonymous export,” “works everywhere” and unverified superiority over competitors.

**Do not use em dashes, including the U+2014 character, anywhere in product copy, documentation or launch assets.** Use a period, colon, comma or ordinary hyphen when punctuation is needed. Keep the voice direct, useful and calm.

## Asset consistency

All screenshots must show the real interface using fictional browsing data. Omit the optional donation strip and support links from promotional captures through capture-only cropping or styling; keep them intact in the actual extension. Preserve the exact original HistoryOut wordmark, favicon and extension icon. Fresh promotional and thumbnail compositions must incorporate those original assets. Do not market the product as HistoryOut 2; keep version numbers in release notes and technical metadata. Capture the actual output file when showing export results. Brand completion does not establish release completion. Label an unreleased build a release candidate, and distinguish a real recorded demonstration from a script or animated preview.
